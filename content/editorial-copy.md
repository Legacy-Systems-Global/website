## 5. A LETTER FROM OUR FOUNDERS

**To our shareholders, our neighbors, and the many people who did not choose us —**

Every year I sit down at the same oak desk, in the same quiet office, to tell you plainly how your company is doing. I am not a fancy man. I have driven the same car for thirty years, though not always in the same direction, and never for the same reason twice.

People ask me the secret. There isn't one, and I'd tell you if there were, and then I'd own it. The truth is simpler than the business schools make it. You find a wonderful business — one with a moat so wide the customer can't get out — and you buy the whole thing, and you never, ever sell it. Selling is for people who need money. We are not people who need money. We are the reason money needs us.

Now, I read the papers. Some of them we own. And I know there is a certain kind of person who looks at a company like ours and gets nervous. To that person I say: relax. We have never done anything that wasn't, at the time, in some jurisdiction, technically fine. When the rules changed, we changed jurisdictions. That's not evasion. That's respect for the rules — we take them so seriously we go where they can't reach us and admire them from a distance.

A word on our people. They are the heart of Legacy Systems, and we monitor that heart closely, because a heart that skips a beat is a heart worth understanding. We ask a great deal of them. We ask that they log their hours, mind their colleagues, and treat the PO Box as a member of the family. In return we offer stability, purpose, and a benefits package that vests, God willing, before they do.

I won't pretend this year was without its challenges. A regulator retired. A competitor also retired, more suddenly. "Just" Vitamins had what the lawyers call an "event," and what I call Tuesday. Through it all, our free cash flow did what it always does: it flowed, freely, toward us.

I am often asked what I'm afraid of. Death, mostly — which is why we structured the company so it doesn't apply to us. Legacy Systems has no natural end. It is not a man; it cannot die. It is a system, and systems, well-tended, run forever. That is the gift I hope to leave you. Not wealth. Wealth is temporary. **Continuity** is forever.

Thank you for your trust. It was never really optional, and I'm grateful for it all the same.

Sincerely, **The Founders** *(names withheld per §12)*

## 6. CORPORATE GOVERNANCE

**Headline:** A framework for accountability.

**Intro:** Legacy Systems is committed to the highest standards of corporate governance. Our structure is designed to ensure independence, oversight, and rigorous checks and balances at every level, all of which report, ultimately, to the same person.

**Board Independence:** Our Board comprises a majority of independent directors, independent in the sense that they are financially independent because of us, and would prefer to remain so. No director serves on any committee that could plausibly inconvenience the company.

**Committees of the Board:**

- **Audit Committee** — Reviews the numbers we show it.
- **Compensation Committee** — Determines what everyone is worth, generously, starting at the top.
- **Nominating & Perpetuity Committee** — Ensures leadership continuity across natural and unnatural transitions.
- **Risk Committee** — Meets annually to confirm the risk has been transferred to a Special Purpose Vehicle.

**Shareholder Rights:** Legacy Systems maintains a dual-class share structure. Class A shares carry one vote. Class B shares carry the remaining votes. Class B shares are not for sale, were never for sale, and are held by an entity whose name is itself privileged.

**Ethics & Conduct:** Every director, officer, and employee is bound by our Code of Conduct, a living document, in that it changes retroactively as needed to reflect what has already occurred.

**Pull quote:** *"Good governance is not about limiting power. It's about documenting it beautifully."*

## 9. CAPITAL ALLOCATION PHILOSOPHY

**Headline:** Where the money goes, it stays.

**Manifesto:** Capital allocation is the only job. Everything else is decoration.

We rank every dollar by its opportunity to compound, unimpeded, in perpetuity. We invest first in our existing businesses, second in the acquisition of new ones, and never in the return of capital to those who provided it. To hand money back is to admit we found nothing better to do with it. We always find something better to do with it.

**Our hierarchy of capital:**

1. Acquire wonderful businesses at fair prices.
2. Acquire fair businesses at wonderful prices.
3. Acquire the businesses that were going to compete with (1) and (2).
4. Acquire the mechanism by which prices are determined.
5. Repeat, forever, quietly.

**We do not:** pay dividends, buy back stock as a favor, or explain a bad year. There has never been a bad year. There have been years described, later, as formative.
