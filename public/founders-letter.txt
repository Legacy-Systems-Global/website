LEGACY SYSTEMS — A LETTER FROM OUR FOUNDERS

To our shareholders, our neighbors, and the many people who did not choose us —

Every year I sit down at the same oak desk, in the same quiet office, to tell you plainly how your company is doing. I am not a fancy man. I have driven the same car for thirty years, though not always in the same direction, and never for the same reason twice.

People ask me the secret. There isn't one, and I'd tell you if there were, and then I'd own it. The truth is simpler than the business schools make it. You find a wonderful business — one with a moat so wide the customer can't get out — and you buy the whole thing, and you never, ever sell it. Selling is for people who need money. We are not people who need money. We are the reason money needs us.

Now, I read the papers. Some of them we own. And I know there is a certain kind of person who looks at a company like ours and gets nervous. To that person I say: relax. We have never done anything that wasn't, at the time, in some jurisdiction, technically fine. When the rules changed, we changed jurisdictions. That's not evasion. That's respect for the rules — we take them so seriously we go where they can't reach us and admire them from a distance.

A word on our people. They are the heart of Legacy Systems, and we monitor that heart closely, because a heart that skips a beat is a heart worth understanding. We ask a great deal of them. We ask that they log their hours, mind their colleagues, and treat the PO Box as a member of the family. In return we offer stability, purpose, and a benefits package that vests, God willing, before they do.

I won't pretend this year was without its challenges. A regulator retired. A competitor also retired, more suddenly. "Just" Vitamins had what the lawyers call an "event," and what I call Tuesday. Through it all, our free cash flow did what it always does: it flowed, freely, toward us.

I am often asked what I'm afraid of. Death, mostly — which is why we structured the company so it doesn't apply to us. Legacy Systems has no natural end. It is not a man; it cannot die. It is a system, and systems, well-tended, run forever. That is the gift I hope to leave you. Not wealth. Wealth is temporary. Continuity is forever.

Thank you for your trust. It was never really optional, and I'm grateful for it all the same.

Sincerely, The Founders (names withheld per §12)
